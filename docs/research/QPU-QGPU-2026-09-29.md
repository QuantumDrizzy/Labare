# Research note — what a QPU rung and a QGPU rung can honestly be (2026-09-29)

Question: MTLB has its CPU milestone. What can the QPU and QGPU rungs contribute, held to the
same discipline as QuBLAR and LYTH: pre-registered falsifiers, independent oracles, real data
where it exists?

## 1. What we already measured (our own repositories)

- **A faster state-vector kernel is not the contribution.** `RESEARCH/gate-fusion`, ADR-0001,
  2026-09-14, on the RTX 5060 Ti:
  - cuStateVec applies fused gates up to m = 5 qubits at 99–103 % of the memory bandwidth
    measured in the same process (378.3 GB/s).
  - The memory system is saturated, and the repository closed with no kernel.
  - Above m = 5 it falls off a cliff (3.32× at m = 6). An optimal schedule never picks 6-qubit
    groups unless a kernel gets them under 1.5×.
- **What already exists on the gate side.**
  - LYTH runs a Hadamard on the top qubit bit-exact on PTX and on MTLB (`examples/hadamard.lyth`).
  - LYTH ADR-0028 (Proposed) would open every single-qubit gate on every qubit without a copy.
- **What already exists on the tensor side.** MTLB has `ZIPPER` and `ZIPPER2`: MPS transfer-matrix
  steps at χ = 1 and 2, with int8 cores. It also has a golden, `<GHZ'_4|i+^4>`, which catches 6 of
  8 injected faults, and a random-MPS suite that catches all 8.
- **Oracles on this machine.** Qiskit 2.2.3 (`quantum_info.Statevector`, a pure-NumPy reference)
  runs on Windows Python 3.11. cuQuantum runs in the WSL venv (Linux wheels only).

## 2. What exists outside

- **Real QPUs you can use for free.** The IBM Quantum Open Plan gives 10 minutes per rolling
  28 days. Heron r2 `ibm_kingston` (156 qubits, 340k CLOPS, median two-qubit error about
  2.0e-3) is open to Open Plan users [1][2]. It is the only real hardware reachable at zero cost.
  **It needs an account the user creates himself.**
- **The frontier is the control plane.**
  - eQASM (TU Delft, HPCA 2019) exists because a real QPU is limited by the instruction issue
    rate and by feedback latency: from a measurement result to a conditioned operation [3].
  - NVIDIA NVQLink and cudaq-realtime (2026) put GPU↔QPU round trips at about 2–4 µs and do
    real-time QEC decoding inside the loop [4][5].
  - Dynamic circuits (mid-circuit measurement plus feed-forward) are what make teleportation,
    repeat-until-success and error correction possible on hardware. The classical decision has
    to land inside the qubits' coherence time.

## 3. Where MTLB can contribute (the thesis to test)

MTLB is a CPU with a **cost model**: it counts what every instruction costs. A QPU rung built
on it can therefore answer a question simulators do not ask.

> Given a dynamic circuit, does its classical feed-forward fit inside the coherence window of
> the qubits it waits on, and what fidelity follows from the answer?

That is falsifiable against real hardware, since IBM publishes per-qubit T1, T2 and
gate/readout durations. It is also where GPUs actually meet QPUs in 2026.

- **The QPU rung.** Quantum instructions in the ISA, dispatched to interchangeable backends:
  - an exact host state vector, as the reference;
  - cuStateVec on the GPU, used and not competed with, since the roofline is already measured;
  - later, a real QPU through OpenQASM 3.
- **The QGPU rung.** The same instructions on an MPS backend, which is what the GPU can hold
  when a state vector cannot. The `=` gate between them is QGPU = QPU: on circuits whose
  entanglement fits χ, the MPS amplitudes equal the state vector's. Where it does not fit, the
  truncation error is reported, never hidden.

## 4. Phasing

1. **U-QPU phase 1, the reference.**
   - ISA instructions and an exact host backend.
   - Gates against closed forms, and circuits against an independent oracle (Qiskit).
   - Measurement with feed-forward in the ISA (teleportation).
   - ADR-0003.
2. **Phase 2, timing.** Quantum instructions get durations from a device calibration (IBM
   Heron), and classical instructions get a declared clock. The cost model predicts
   feed-forward latency against T2. Then the prediction is checked on `ibm_kingston`, which
   needs the user's account.
3. **QGPU.** An MPS backend behind the same instructions (ZIPPER2 and LYTH ADR-0028), with the
   QGPU = QPU equality gate.
4. **GPU backend.** cuStateVec in WSL. We measure dispatch overhead against the 378 GB/s
   roofline. We do not claim speed.
